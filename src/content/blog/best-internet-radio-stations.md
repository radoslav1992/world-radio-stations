## Stations born on the internet

### 1. Radio Paradise

Bill and Rebecca Goldsmith started Radio Paradise in Paradise, California, in 2000. It is still independent and listener-funded, with no adverts and no subscription. Every set is sequenced by hand, so a folk song can slide into electronica without a jolt.

There are four mixes, Main, Mellow, Rock and Global, and all four come in lossless FLAC as well as standard streams.

**Best for:** all-day eclectic listening with no ads. Stream it live on its [station guide](/stations/radio-paradise).

### 2. SomaFM

SomaFM began as a micropower broadcast at Burning Man in 1999, and Rusty Hodge relaunched it as a full-time internet station in San Francisco in February 2000. He still runs it. Instead of one stream, it offers more than two dozen commercial-free channels, each covering a narrow mood or genre such as ambient, downtempo, lounge or indie pop. Listeners pay the bills.

**Best for:** a channel that fits exactly one mood, which makes it ideal for work and [coding](/moods/coding). Pick the United States in the [player](/#player) and type "SomaFM" into the search box, or browse the [US stations page](/country/us).

### 3. NTS Radio

Femi Adeyemi started NTS on a tiny budget, and its first broadcast went out on 4 April 2011 from Gillett Square in Dalston, east London. Its hosts are now spread around the world, but it still sounds more like a DJ booth than a radio station, with long specialist shows covering jazz, ambient, club music and experimental sound.

NTS runs two live channels around the clock. It also has a set of themed "Infinite Mixtapes" drawn from its archive, such as Poolside and Slow Focus. Listening is free. An optional NTS Supporters membership adds extras such as live tracklists.

**Best for:** underground, leftfield and DJ-led music. Listen at nts.live or in its app, or look for it on our [United Kingdom page](/country/gb).

### 4. Jazz24

Jazz24 is a 24-hour, listener-funded jazz stream from KNKX Public Radio in the Seattle–Tacoma area, part of the NPR Network. Expect standards, vocalists, and classic and contemporary players, with short announcements instead of long talk. Its new app, launched in November 2025, works with Apple CarPlay and Android Auto.

**Best for:** easygoing jazz, day and night. Search for it in the [player](/#player), browse more in our [Jazz & Soul collection](/moods/jazz), or see our list of the [best jazz radio stations](/blog/best-jazz-radio-stations).

## Broadcasters with world-class streams

### 5. FIP

FIP is part of the French public broadcaster Radio France and has been on air since 1971. It is famous for long runs of music with almost no talk and no commercials: jazz can follow chanson, and a film score can come next.

Online, FIP adds themed webradios that share the same curatorial ear. They include FIP Jazz, FIP Groove, FIP Rock, FIP Electro, FIP Reggae, FIP Pop, FIP Monde (world music) and FIP Nouveautés (new releases).

**Best for:** genre streams chosen by people with real taste. Stream it live on its [station guide](/stations/fip).

### 6. KEXP

KEXP started out in 1972 as KCMU, a college station linked to the University of Washington, and is now a listener-supported non-profit on 90.3 FM in Seattle. Its DJs choose their own music, leaning towards new and independent artists, and its filmed in-studio sessions on YouTube have won it fans worldwide.

**Best for:** hearing new bands before everyone else does. Stream it live on its [station guide](/stations/kexp).

### 7. KCRW

KCRW is the public station on 89.9 FM in Santa Monica, licensed to Santa Monica College. Its music show *Morning Becomes Eclectic* has launched many new artists, and the station mixes music with NPR news. For music only, its online channel Eclectic24 plays picks from KCRW's DJs around the clock.

**Best for:** tastemaker music from Los Angeles. Stream it live on its [station guide](/stations/kcrw).

### 8. WFMU

WFMU broadcasts on 91.1 FM from Jersey City, across the Hudson from Manhattan, and is often called the longest-running freeform station in the United States: each DJ controls their own show, with no station playlist. It takes no corporate sponsorship; listener pledges and its annual record fair pay the bills.

**Best for:** gloriously unpredictable radio with real personalities. Find it on the [US stations page](/country/us) or search "WFMU" in the [player](/#player).

### 9. BBC Radio 6 Music

BBC Radio 6 Music launched in 2002 as a digital-only station and still has no FM or AM frequency. It covers alternative music in the widest sense, from indie and electronic to soul and hip-hop, and digs into the BBC's archive of live sessions. Listening abroad? The BBC closed its BBC Sounds app to users outside the UK in July 2025 and now points them to BBC.com.

**Best for:** alternative music with expert presenters. Stream it live on its [station guide](/stations/bbc-radio-6-music).

### 10. FM4

[FM4](/stations/fm4) is the Austrian broadcaster ORF's alternative station. It launched in Vienna in 1995 and switches between German and English, with daytime news in English. Its evening specialist shows go deep into house, techno, punk and more.

**Best for:** European indie and electronic music, with plenty of English you can follow. Stream it live on its [station guide](/stations/fm4).

### 11. triple j

Australia's public youth station began in Sydney in 1975 as 2JJ. [triple j](/stations/triple-j) now broadcasts nationally and focuses on new and alternative music, much of it Australian. Its *Like A Version* covers and its listener-voted Hottest 100 countdown are national institutions.

**Best for:** new Australian music and alternative hits. Stream it live on its [station guide](/stations/triple-j), and find more in our guide to the [best radio stations in Australia](/blog/best-radio-stations-australia).

### 12. Radio Swiss Jazz

[Radio Swiss Jazz](/stations/radio-swiss-jazz) is run by the Swiss public broadcaster SRG SSR. It plays jazz, blues and soul 24 hours a day with no adverts, no talk shows and no news bulletins, only short announcements of each artist and title.

**Best for:** uninterrupted jazz for work or dinner. Stream it live on its [station guide](/stations/radio-swiss-jazz).

### 13. WQXR

[WQXR](/stations/wqxr), on 105.9 FM, is New York City's classical station. Founded in 1936, it is one of the oldest classical broadcasters in the US and is now run by New York Public Radio.

**Best for:** classical music from a station with deep roots. Stream it live on its [station guide](/stations/wqxr), or see our pick of the [best classical radio stations](/blog/best-classical-radio-stations).

### 14. BBC World Service

The [BBC World Service](/stations/bbc-world-service) began in 1932 as the BBC Empire Service. Today it broadcasts news in English and dozens of other languages. In audience figures published in July 2026, the BBC said World Service output reached 355 million people a week across radio, TV and online.

**Best for:** international news in English, around the clock. Stream it live on its [station guide](/stations/bbc-world-service).

## What makes a good internet radio station

- **People, not algorithms.** Every station here is programmed by DJs or a music team, which is what separates radio from an automated playlist.
- **Good stream quality.** Bitrate, measured in kilobits per second (kbps), sets how good a stream sounds. Most stations stream at 96–192 kbps, and 128 kbps already sounds good on headphones. AAC sounds better than MP3 at the same bitrate. Lossless FLAC, like Radio Paradise's, sounds best but uses far more data. Our player shows each station's bitrate when the station publishes it.
- **No sign-up.** You shouldn't need an account just to press play. None of the stations above needs one in our player.
- **Few or no ads.** Radio Paradise, SomaFM, WFMU, FIP and Radio Swiss Jazz carry no commercials. Commercial stations play adverts, and some streams add an extra ad when you connect.
- **A reliable, secure stream.** Browsers block streams that aren't served over HTTPS, so our web player shows only the part of the catalogue that plays safely in a browser. You can read more in [how internet radio works](/blog/how-internet-radio-works).

## FAQ

### Are online radio stations free?

Yes. Almost every online radio station is free to listen to. Listener-supported stations such as Radio Paradise, SomaFM and WFMU ask for voluntary donations. Our player is free too, with no subscription or sign-up.

### Do I need an app to listen to internet radio?

No. A web browser is enough, on a computer or a phone, including Safari on iPhone. Apps add extras such as car integration, but you don't need one: open the [player](/#player) and press play.

### How much data does internet radio use?

About 45–90 MB per hour, depending on stream quality: most stations stream at 96–192 kbps, and a 128 kbps stream uses roughly 58 MB an hour. Lossless streams use much more. On Wi-Fi, no mobile data is used. Our comparison of [FM and internet radio](/blog/fm-vs-internet-radio) has more detail.

### Where can I listen to world radio online for free?
