The best radio stations for studying play steady, mostly instrumental music with few ads and little talk. Our top picks are Radio Swiss Classic and Radio Swiss Jazz for uninterrupted classical and jazz, FIP for gentle variety, SomaFM’s Groove Salad and Drone Zone for ambient focus, a lo-fi hip hop stream for coding, and BBC Radio 3 when you want classical with a human touch. If you just want to press play, start with our [Focus collection](/moods/focus).

## What makes a good study station

Music can help you settle into work, but the wrong track at the wrong moment pulls your attention away. The stations that work best share a few traits:

- **Few or no vocals.** Lyrics compete with the words you are reading. A 2018 meta-analysis by Vasilev and colleagues found that background music with lyrics hurt reading comprehension, while instrumental music had little measurable effect either way.
- **Little talk and few ads.** A presenter or a loud advert breaks concentration as surely as a song you love.
- **Consistent energy.** No sudden jumps from quiet to loud.
- **Long-form flow.** Ambient, classical, jazz, lo-fi and downtempo all work well.

Everyone is different, so treat the list below as a starting point and keep what works for you.

## The best radio stations for studying

### 1. Radio Swiss Classic

A classical music station run by the Swiss public broadcaster SRG SSR. It is free of advertising and designed for background listening: just music, with short spoken introductions and closing announcements rather than chatty presenters. It broadcasts on DAB+ in Switzerland and streams worldwide. Find it on the [Switzerland page](/country/ch).

**Best for:** long reading sessions and revision where you want calm with no interruptions.

### 2. Radio Swiss Jazz

Its sister station plays jazz, blues and soul around the clock, again without ads and with only brief announcements. The mix includes vocal jazz, so it suits writing and practical work better than close reading. Stream it live on its [station guide](/stations/radio-swiss-jazz).

**Best for:** essays, coursework and creative tasks where classical feels too formal.

### 3. FIP

The Radio France music station is famous for eclectic, hand-picked programming — jazz, world music, classical, rock and electronic — with very little talk between tracks. If the main station’s variety is too much, Radio France also streams themed FIP channels such as FIP Jazz and FIP Groove. Listen on the [FIP station guide](/stations/fip).

**Best for:** routine work like notes, flashcards and admin, when you want variety without chatter.

### 4. SomaFM Groove Salad

SomaFM is an independent, listener-supported internet radio station that Rusty Hodge launched from San Francisco in 2000. It runs dozens of commercial-free channels; Groove Salad plays chilled ambient and downtempo beats, mostly instrumental. Stream it on SomaFM’s website or apps, or try searching for it in our [player](/#player).

**Best for:** coding, problem sets and long sessions at a steady pace.

### 5. SomaFM Drone Zone (and Deep Space One)

For the least distraction possible, Drone Zone serves atmospheric textures with minimal beats. Its sibling Deep Space One goes for deep ambient, experimental and space music, with a little more movement.

**Best for:** memorising, close reading and anything that needs total concentration.

### 6. Lo-fi hip hop streams

Lo-fi hip hop — soft, looping beats with a dusty, vinyl feel — is closely tied to studying thanks to one YouTube channel. Founded as ChilledCow, it started its round-the-clock lo-fi stream in February 2017 and was renamed Lofi Girl in 2021, after the animated studying girl in its artwork. For lo-fi in a radio player, open our [Coding & lo-fi collection](/moods/coding), which gathers stations tagged lo-fi from around the world.

**Best for:** coding and homework where you want a gentle beat to keep you moving.

### 7. BBC Radio 3

The BBC’s classical station broadcasts across the UK on 90.2–92.4 FM and on DAB. It plays classical music from early music to new commissions, along with jazz, world music and complete live concerts. It has presenters and some speech, so it’s less hands-off than the Swiss stations, but its daytime music sequences are excellent company. Stream it live on its [station guide](/stations/bbc-radio-3). Outside the UK, which BBC streams you can hear varies; see [how to listen to radio from another country](/blog/how-to-listen-radio-from-another-country).

**Best for:** daytime study when you’d like some human company between pieces.

### 8. Radio Paradise Mellow Mix

[Radio Paradise](/stations/radio-paradise) is a listener-supported, ad-free internet station whose playlists are chosen by people, not algorithms. Its Mellow Mix channel keeps things relaxed, and it’s one of the few stations that offers lossless streams. Expect vocals, so save it for lighter tasks.

**Best for:** tidying notes, planning and other low-pressure work.

### Also worth a try

[WQXR](/stations/wqxr), New York’s noncommercial classical station on 105.9 FM, and the UK’s [Classic FM](/stations/classic-fm-uk) on 100–102 FM are both good for classical, though they have more presenter talk, and Classic FM carries ads. For more ideas, see our guides to the [best classical radio stations](/blog/best-classical-radio-stations) and the [best jazz radio stations](/blog/best-jazz-radio-stations).

## Tips for studying with radio

- **Use the sleep timer as a study timer.** Set the player’s timer for 30, 60 or 90 minutes; when the music stops, take a break.
- **Star your focus stations.** Add the ones that work to your [favourites](/favorites) and start a session in one tap.
- **Keep the volume low.** Music should sit in the background, not compete with your thoughts.
- **Choose ad-free stations for deep work.** Radio Swiss Classic, Radio Swiss Jazz and SomaFM’s listener-supported channels have no ad breaks.
- **Use Wi-Fi when you can.** A typical 128 kbps stream uses about 58 MB an hour.

## FAQ

### What is the best radio station for studying?

For uninterrupted classical music, Radio Swiss Classic. For ambient focus, SomaFM’s Groove Salad or Drone Zone. For coding, a lo-fi hip hop stream. All are free; open the [Focus collection](/moods/focus) to sample several quickly.

### Is it better to study with music or in silence?

It depends on the task and the person. Research suggests music with lyrics tends to hurt reading comprehension, while instrumental music has little effect, so if you study with music, choose instrumental stations for reading-heavy work.

### Is lo-fi good for studying?

Many people find it helps: lo-fi is mostly instrumental, steady in tempo and quiet in the mix. Try our [Coding & lo-fi collection](/moods/coding).

### Is there focus radio with no talking or ads?

Yes. Radio Swiss Classic and Radio Swiss Jazz have no ads and only short announcements, and SomaFM’s channels are commercial-free.
