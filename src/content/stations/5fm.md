5FM is South Africa's national English-language youth station, run by the public broadcaster SABC. Playing pop, hip hop and a heavy dose of dance music, it has soundtracked young South African life for five decades — and given the country's world-conquering electronic scene a national stage.

## History

The station launched in 1975 as Radio 5, an English pop service of the SABC, and rebranded as 5FM in the 1990s as South Africa's media reinvented itself with democracy. Through the transition it evolved from a segregated-era pop outlet into a deliberately inclusive youth brand, its line-ups mixing the country's diverse voices and accents. Generations of famous South African DJs and presenters passed through its studios, and the station's support for local music intensified as homegrown genres — kwaito, SA hip hop, and later the house lineages that produced amapiano — took over the national soundtrack.

## Format and programming

Daytimes carry current pop and hip hop with big personality shows; nights and weekends belong to dance music, where 5FM has long championed South African house and electronic producers. Local-music quotas are embraced rather than endured, and the station's live broadcasts from festivals and its annual events keep it embedded in youth culture across the country.

## Reach

5FM broadcasts on FM transmitters covering South Africa's cities and major routes and streams online, complementing SABC sister stations that serve the country's other ten official languages.

## Significance

5FM is where South African youth radio went national — a public station that helped carry the country's electronic music from township parties to global charts, and a rare English-language thread connecting young listeners across a famously diverse nation.
