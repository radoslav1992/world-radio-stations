BBC Radio 1Xtra is the BBC's national digital station devoted to Black music: hip hop, grime, UK rap, R&B, dancehall, Afrobeats and the club sounds in between. Sitting alongside Radio 1 in the corporation's youth portfolio, it has become the most important broadcast platform for British Black music.

## History

1Xtra launched on 16 August 2002 as one of the BBC's first digital-only networks, created after years of criticism that British Black music was confined to pirate radio. It arrived at a fortunate moment: grime was about to erupt from East London, and 1Xtra's specialist shows gave the scene national airtime that commercial radio wouldn't. Over the following two decades the station played a central role in taking UK rap and grime from pirate aerials to arena headliners, championing artists like Stormzy, Dave and Little Simz on their way up.

## Format and programming

Daytime shows mix current hip hop, R&B and Afrobeats with entertainment and youth-focused news from the BBC's newsroom, while evenings and weekends go deep on specialist scenes — grime, drill, dancehall, gospel and UK garage — with DJs who are themselves fixtures of those communities. Live Lounge-style sessions, freestyles and documentary strands round out a distinctly public-service take on street-level music.

## Reach

1Xtra broadcasts across the UK on DAB and worldwide through BBC Sounds. Its cultural reach — through viral freestyles, sessions and social clips — extends far beyond its broadcast audience.

## Significance

1Xtra gave British Black music the national infrastructure pirate radio could never provide, and its two decades of championing homegrown scenes helped UK rap and grime become the country's dominant youth music. It remains the reference point for the sound of young Britain.
