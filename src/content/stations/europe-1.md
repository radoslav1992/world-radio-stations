Europe 1 is one of the great names of French radio — a généraliste station whose swaggering, modern style upended European broadcasting in the 1950s and 60s. Though its audience has declined from historic peaks, its place in radio history is untouchable.

## History

Europe n°1 began broadcasting in January 1955 from a transmitter at Felsberg in Germany's Saarland, skirting the French state broadcasting monopoly as a "peripheral" station. It sounded like nothing else in France: fast, informal, journalist-driven. Its youth program "Salut les copains" ignited the yé-yé pop explosion of the 1960s, and its news coverage — notably during May 1968, when its reporters broadcast live from the barricades — set standards for French broadcast journalism. Long the jewel of the Lagardère group, the station struggled through the 2010s with falling ratings and, after Vivendi's takeover of Lagardère, moved editorially closer to the Bolloré media stable.

## Format and programming

Today Europe 1 programs morning news and interviews, opinion-led talk, entertainment and cultural shows, with close cross-promotion with the CNews television channel. Archival treasures from its golden decades remain a French cultural reference.

## Reach

The station broadcasts on FM nationwide, on DAB+ and online. Its audience, while a fraction of RTL's or France Inter's, remains significant, and its podcast archive keeps its heritage in circulation.

## Significance

Europe 1 modernized French radio: it invented the country's style of star interviewers, broke the state's monopoly grip in practice, and gave French pop culture its first broadcast home. Few stations anywhere can claim as much influence on how a nation sounds.
