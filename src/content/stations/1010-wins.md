1010 WINS is New York City's original all-news radio station and one of the most recognizable brands in American broadcasting. Its promise — "You give us 22 minutes, we'll give you the world" — and the teletype rhythm pulsing behind its anchors have been part of the city's soundtrack for six decades.

## History

WINS spent the 1950s and early 1960s as a top-40 powerhouse — it was here that DJ Alan Freed popularized the phrase "rock and roll" for the New York audience. On 19 April 1965, owner Westinghouse flipped the station to something almost untested: news, all day, every day. The gamble made WINS one of the first all-news stations in the United States and established the format's conventions — the rotating news wheel, traffic and weather together, the insistent urgency. In 2022 the station added an FM simulcast on 92.3, bringing the format to the FM band under owner Audacy.

## Format and programming

The WINS wheel packs headlines, traffic, weather, business and sports into a tight, repeating cycle designed for listeners who tune in for exactly as long as they need. Coverage leans hard on the five boroughs: city politics, transit, crime and the daily mechanics of New York life, with reporters live around the city.

## Reach

1010 AM is a 50,000-watt clear-channel signal that blankets the New York area and carries far beyond it at night, complemented by the 92.3 FM simulcast and streaming through Audacy.

## Significance

WINS proved that a great city would sustain nonstop news radio, and every all-news station since — including its own longtime rival WCBS 880 — followed the template it set in 1965. It remains the reflex station New Yorkers reach for when something happens.
