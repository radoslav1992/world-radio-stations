SomaFM is one of the great originals of internet radio: an independent, listener-supported broadcaster from San Francisco that has streamed carefully curated, commercial-free music since 2000. It exists only online — no transmitter, no ads, no corporate owner — and has built a fiercely loyal worldwide audience.

## History

Rusty Hodge launched SomaFM from his basement in the South of Market district (the "Soma" of the name) in 2000, starting with the downtempo channel Groove Salad. The station survived the 2002 internet-radio royalty crisis — which briefly forced it off the air and made Hodge a prominent advocate for webcaster rights — and returned to grow into a network of more than thirty channels, funded entirely by listener donations.

## Format and programming

Each SomaFM channel is a distinct, human-curated world: Groove Salad's ambient downtempo, Drone Zone's deep ambient, Secret Agent's spy-movie lounge, Indie Pop Rocks, Underground 80s, the winter-only Christmas Lounge and many more. There are no commercials and almost no interruptions; the curation itself is the product, and channels are programmed by DJs with decades of crate-digging behind them.

## Reach

SomaFM streams globally through its website and apps and is built into countless internet radio devices and players. Its channels — especially Groove Salad — are default background listening for studios, offices and late-night coders around the world.

## Significance

SomaFM proved that independent internet radio could sustain itself on listener support alone, years before "subscription audio" became an industry. Its channels have defined entire micro-genres of listening, and its survival through the royalty battles of the early 2000s helped preserve the legal space in which all small webcasters now operate.
