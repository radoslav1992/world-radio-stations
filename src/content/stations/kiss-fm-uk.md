KISS is the United Kingdom's flagship station for dance music, hip hop and R&B. It carries the rare distinction of having started as an illegal broadcaster and ending up a national institution — the London pirate that won a licence and never looked back.

## History

Kiss FM began in 1985 as a South London pirate station playing the Black music the legal dial ignored, with DJs including future superstars like Trevor Nelson and Judge Jules. After a high-profile campaign, it won a legal licence and relaunched on 1 September 1990 on 100.0 FM — the first legal station of its kind in London. Through the 1990s it was the broadcast heart of UK rave, jungle, garage and house culture. Now owned by Bauer, the brand spans a family of digital stations, including the old-skool and anthems service KISSTORY, itself one of the UK's most popular digital stations.

## Format and programming

Daytimes mix current dance, pop-rhythmic and hip hop hits; nights belong to specialist DJs covering house, drum & bass, garage, Afrobeats and grime. The KISS Breakfast show targets a young, urban audience, and weekend mix shows keep the station's DJ heritage front and centre.

## Reach

KISS broadcasts on 100.0 FM in London, in East Anglia on heritage frequencies, and nationally on DAB, with KISSTORY and KISS Fresh alongside. Streaming and its app extend the brand's reach across the UK and beyond.

## Significance

KISS legitimized British dance-music radio: it took pirate culture mainstream without entirely sanding off its edges, trained a generation of the UK's most famous DJs, and demonstrated the commercial power of Black and dance music formats that the industry had underestimated for decades.
