NPO 3FM is the Dutch public broadcaster's station for pop and new music — the channel where the Netherlands' young listeners have traditionally discovered what comes next. Its December charity marathon, Serious Request, is one of the most successful radio fundraising events ever devised.

## History

The station began on 11 October 1965 as Hilversum 3, the public system's answer to the wildly popular offshore pirates like Radio Veronica that were pulling Dutch youth away from official radio. Renamed Radio 3 and later 3FM, it became the country's tastemaker station, its DJs national celebrities. In 2004 it launched "3FM Serious Request": DJs locked in a glass house without food, broadcasting around the clock while the public donates to a Red Cross cause — a format so effective it raised tens of millions of euros and was copied in a dozen countries.

## Format and programming

3FM plays current pop, indie, dance and Dutch acts with a public-service emphasis on new and homegrown music — its "Serious Talent" strand has boosted generations of Dutch artists. Personality-led shows, live sessions and festival broadcasts (including from Lowlands and Pinkpop) keep it embedded in Dutch music culture even as its ratings battle streaming-era headwinds.

## Reach

The station broadcasts nationwide on FM and DAB+ and streams through NPO's platforms; Serious Request still commands the whole country's attention each December.

## Significance

3FM is the Netherlands' institutional bridge between public broadcasting and pop culture — the station that answered the pirates in the sixties and invented one of radio's great charity formats forty years later.
