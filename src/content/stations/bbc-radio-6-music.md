BBC Radio 6 Music is the BBC's digital-only network dedicated to alternative music. Its playlist ranges across indie, rock, electronic, soul, funk, hip hop and experimental music, drawing heavily on the BBC's session archive alongside new releases.

## History

6 Music launched on 11 March 2002 as the first new national BBC radio network in decades, created for the digital platforms of DAB and online rather than analogue broadcast. In 2010 the BBC proposed closing the station as part of a strategic review, prompting a high-profile public campaign by listeners and musicians; the plan was dropped, and the publicity helped drive substantial audience growth. 6 Music has since become the most successful digital-only station in the UK, with a weekly audience that surpasses some analogue networks.

## Format and programming

The station targets music enthusiasts rather than a demographic defined by age, mixing new alternative releases with classic album tracks and rarities. Presenters are given unusual freedom over their playlists, and the schedule features artist interviews, live sessions and themed shows across genres from psychedelia and punk to electronica and jazz. Material from the BBC's decades of recorded sessions, including the John Peel archive, is a distinctive part of its output, and events such as the 6 Music Festival have showcased live performance.

## Reach

6 Music broadcasts across the United Kingdom on DAB digital radio and streams worldwide through BBC Sounds. It has no analogue FM or AM frequency, and its strong performance helped validate the BBC's investment in digital radio.

## Significance

6 Music occupies a niche that commercial radio rarely serves, championing alternative and heritage music with public service depth. The 2010 campaign to save it became a landmark moment in UK broadcasting, demonstrating the loyalty digital stations could command, and the network remains an influential tastemaker for new alternative artists.
