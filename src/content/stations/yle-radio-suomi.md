Yle Radio Suomi is Finland's most-listened radio station — the public broadcaster's big, warm-hearted national channel that combines news, regional programming, Finnish music and sport. For a huge share of Finns it is simply "the radio".

## History

Radio Suomi took its current form in 1990, when Yleisradio reorganized its radio services and gathered its regional stations and mainstream programming into one full-service network. The channel inherited the deepest traditions of Finnish public radio — Yle had broadcast since 1926 — and paired them with a populist touch: schlager and Finnish iskelmä music, phone-ins, and the sports commentary that unites the country whenever Finland's ice hockey team takes the ice.

## Format and programming

The day moves through news magazines, regional windows produced by Yle's newsrooms around the country, easy-going music sequences heavy on domestic artists, and evening sport. Radio Suomi's coverage of hockey championships and Olympic moments is the stuff of national memory, and its unhurried overnight programming keeps lights on in farmhouses well above the Arctic Circle.

## Reach

Radio Suomi broadcasts on FM across every region of Finland with local opt-outs, and streams via Yle Areena. Its weekly reach — regularly the largest of any Finnish station — spans generations, though its heartland is the over-45 audience.

## Significance

Radio Suomi shows what a public full-service channel can still be in a small nation: the shared soundtrack of daily life, from morning news to hockey gold. Its blend of regional presence and national ritual has kept it unbeatable in Finnish radio for three decades.
