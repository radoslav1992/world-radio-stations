franceinfo is France's public rolling-news radio station — headlines, reports and analysis around the clock. When it launched it was the first all-news radio station in Europe, and it remains the reflex frequency for French listeners the moment news breaks.

## History

France Info was created on 1 June 1987 within the public broadcaster Radio France, the brainchild of executives inspired by America's all-news formats but determined to build a public-service version. The gamble worked immediately: the station's tight news wheel and authoritative tone made it a fixture of French life. In 2016 it became the radio heart of a broader public news alliance, sharing the "franceinfo" brand (rendered in lowercase) with a television channel and digital platform operated jointly with France Télévisions.

## Format and programming

The station cycles news bulletins with correspondent reports, press reviews, fact-checking segments — its "Vrai ou Faux" strand tackles misinformation daily — plus economics, culture and sport in short, disciplined modules. Election nights and major events bring extended live coverage drawing on Radio France's full newsroom, the largest in French audio.

## Reach

franceinfo broadcasts on FM across France — 105.5 in Paris — on DAB+ and online, where its app and podcasts extend a brand that consistently ranks among the country's most-consumed news sources in any medium.

## Significance

franceinfo proved that all-news radio could be public-service in character: rigorous, ad-light and universally accessible. Nearly four decades on, it is one of Europe's most trusted broadcast news brands and the template other public broadcasters studied when building their own news channels.
