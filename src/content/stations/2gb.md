2GB is Sydney's talk radio powerhouse and the most politically influential commercial radio station in Australia. Its breakfast and morning shows have topped the city's ratings for most of the past two decades, and its hosts' opinions routinely become national news.

## History

2GB began broadcasting in 1926, its call sign reputedly honouring the philosopher Giordano Bruno — a legacy of its founding by the Theosophical Society. Through the twentieth century it evolved into a general talk station, but its modern era began in 2002 when Alan Jones brought his enormous breakfast audience to the station; 2GB promptly seized first place in Sydney and rarely let go. Jones retired from radio in 2020, handing breakfast to Ben Fordham, while Ray Hadley's morning show extended its own record-breaking ratings run until his retirement in 2024. The station is now controlled by Nine Entertainment.

## Format and programming

The format is opinion-led talk: agenda-setting breakfast and morning shows, phone-ins where premiers and prime ministers front up to answer for themselves, afternoon programs on city life, and evenings of sport — 2GB's Continuous Call Team is a rugby league broadcasting institution.

## Reach

2GB broadcasts on 873 AM across Sydney, on DAB+ and online, and its programming is networked to stations along Australia's east coast, multiplying its reach and influence.

## Significance

For better and worse, 2GB defines talkback radio in Australia. Its hosts have been power brokers in state and federal politics, and its ratings dominance in the country's biggest city has made it the model — and the cautionary tale — for opinionated commercial talk across the region.
