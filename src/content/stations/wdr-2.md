WDR 2 is the mass-appeal radio channel of Westdeutscher Rundfunk, the public broadcaster of North Rhine-Westphalia. Serving a state of nearly eighteen million people from Cologne, it combines pop hits with substantial news — and some of the most storied football radio in Germany.

## History

WDR 2 took shape as the broadcaster's popular second programme in the decades after WDR's founding in 1956, evolving into the full-service pop-and-information wave format it carries today. Its geography made it mighty: no German regional station serves more people, and WDR 2 has spent much of its life among the country's two or three most-listened channels. Its sports department helped write the soundtrack of German football — the excited Saturday-afternoon "Konferenz" coverage, switching live between simultaneous Bundesliga matches, is a ritual it shaped for the entire ARD network.

## Format and programming

Weekdays pair melodic pop and classic hits with news every half hour in peak times, political interviews, consumer features and the quick-witted cabaret segments German radio loves. Saturdays belong to football: live Bundesliga coverage anchored from Cologne, with the clubs of the Ruhr and Rhineland — Dortmund, Schalke, Cologne — guaranteeing passionate audiences.

## Reach

WDR 2 broadcasts on a dense FM network across North Rhine-Westphalia with regional news windows, plus DAB+ and streaming, reaching millions daily.

## Significance

WDR 2 is public radio at industrial scale — proof that a regional public station can outdraw national commercial networks. Its football coverage alone secures its place in German broadcasting culture.
