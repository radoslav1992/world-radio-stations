RTL is the biggest radio station in France, a "généraliste" mixing news, talk, entertainment and music that has topped the national audience rankings for most of the modern era. Its history runs straight back to Radio Luxembourg, one of the founding legends of European broadcasting.

## History

Radio Luxembourg began French-language broadcasts in 1933 from the Grand Duchy, using Luxembourg's laissez-faire licensing to beam commercial radio into countries where it was forbidden — the original "peripheral station". Rebranded RTL (Radio Télévision Luxembourg) in 1966, it became a pillar of French daily life, its powerful longwave transmitter at Beidweiler carrying it across the country until the analogue signal was finally retired in 2023. The station anchored the growth of the RTL Group media empire; its French radio operations were folded into Groupe M6 in 2021.

## Format and programming

RTL's schedule is the généraliste formula perfected: a dominant morning news block ("RTL Matin"), star interviewers grilling politicians, service and consumer programming, and long-running entertainment monuments — above all "Les Grosses Têtes", the panel comedy show running since 1977 and hosted since 2014 by Laurent Ruquier. Evenings bring the confessional phone-ins and football coverage that are RTL trademarks.

## Reach

RTL broadcasts on FM across France, on DAB+, and streams through its widely used app, regularly gathering the largest cumulative audience in French radio.

## Significance

RTL shaped commercial broadcasting in Europe before commercial broadcasting was even legal in much of it. Today it remains the reference point of French radio — the station whose morning interview sets the day's political agenda.
