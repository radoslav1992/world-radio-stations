3AW is Melbourne's news and talk institution — the station the city argues with over breakfast. It has led Melbourne's radio ratings with extraordinary consistency for decades, built on an obsessive focus on the city itself.

## History

3AW went to air in February 1932, one of the foundation commercial stations of Australian radio. While formats came and went elsewhere, 3AW settled into news and talk and perfected it, developing a culture of long host tenures and deep local knowledge. Its breakfast program with Ross Stevenson — for decades alongside John Burns, later with Russel Howcroft — has been Melbourne's most-listened breakfast show for a generation, frequently posting the biggest ratings shares of any breakfast program in the country. Once part of the Fairfax stable, the station now sits within Nine Entertainment.

## Format and programming

The schedule runs from the famously witty Breakfast through Neil Mitchell's long-dominant (and since continued) mornings slot of city agenda-setting, to afternoon talk, drive and the evening sports service — 3AW's AFL football coverage is a Melbourne ritual in a city consumed by the game. Everything is relentlessly local: potholes, premiers, and the Victorian obsessions of weather and football.

## Reach

3AW broadcasts on 693 AM across greater Melbourne, on DAB+ and online. Its ratings shares — routinely far ahead of the field — make it one of the strongest heritage stations in the English-speaking world.

## Significance

3AW is the case study in local-first talk radio: no national syndication, no shortcuts, just Melbourne talking to itself extremely well. Its nine-decade run at the heart of the city's daily life has few parallels anywhere.
