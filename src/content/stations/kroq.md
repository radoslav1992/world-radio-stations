KROQ, broadcasting on 106.7 FM in Los Angeles, is the most influential alternative rock station in American history. Self-styled "The World Famous KROQ", it turned new wave, punk and alternative music from imports and curiosities into mainstream American radio formats.

## History

KROQ-FM emerged in 1972 from the remains of the free-form station KPPC. Its defining moment came in the early 1980s, when programmer Rick Carroll introduced the "Roq of the 80s" format — new wave and post-punk played like Top 40 — and DJ Rodney Bingenheimer's "Rodney on the ROQ" show gave first American airplay to acts from the Ramones and Blondie to the Go-Go's. Through the 1990s KROQ was the national tastemaker for grunge and alternative, and its playlist decisions could break a band coast to coast. The "Kevin & Bean" morning show, which ran from 1990 to 2019, launched the careers of Jimmy Kimmel and Adam Carolla.

## Format and programming

KROQ plays current alternative and rock alongside the new wave and 90s catalogue it helped create. Its annual events are institutions in themselves: the KROQ Weenie Roast each summer and Almost Acoustic Christmas each December have hosted nearly every major alternative act of the past three decades.

## Reach

The station covers the Los Angeles basin on 106.7 FM and streams through Audacy's platforms, with a listenership and industry influence that have always extended far beyond Southern California.

## Significance

Without KROQ, alternative rock's path to American mainstream radio is hard to imagine. The station created the modern rock format later adopted across the country, and its history is inseparable from the careers of bands from Depeche Mode and The Cure to Nirvana, Weezer and beyond.
