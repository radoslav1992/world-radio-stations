Radio Nacional de España — RNE — is Spain's public radio service, and its flagship generalist channel, Radio Nacional, delivers news, talk, culture and sport nationwide without a single advertisement. It is the public counterweight to Spain's mighty commercial networks.

## History

RNE was founded in January 1937 in Salamanca, in the middle of the Civil War, and for decades afterward served as the state's official voice — famously, all Spanish stations were once obliged to carry its news bulletins. With democracy, RNE transformed into a modern public broadcaster within RTVE, shedding its monopoly privileges and advertising alike. Its all-news channel arrived alongside specialist networks for classical music (Radio Clásica), pop (Radio 3), regional languages (Ràdio 4) and international service (Radio Exterior), leaving Radio Nacional as the generalist mothership.

## Format and programming

The schedule follows Spain's generalist rhythm: a long morning magazine mixing news and interviews, midday and evening news blocks, cultural programming with a depth commercial rivals rarely attempt, and weekend sports coverage including La Liga. Freedom from advertising gives its journalism a measured tone, and its archives — nearly ninety years of Spanish history on tape — are a national treasure.

## Reach

Radio Nacional broadcasts on FM and AM across all of Spain and its islands, on DAB+ and through RTVE's digital platforms, complemented by Radio Exterior's shortwave and satellite service to the Spanish-speaking world.

## Significance

RNE anchors the public side of one of Europe's most vibrant radio markets. While Cadena SER and COPE fight the ratings war, Radio Nacional guarantees that Spanish listeners always have a commercial-free source of record — a role it has played through war, dictatorship and democracy.
