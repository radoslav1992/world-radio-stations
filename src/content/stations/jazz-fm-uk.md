Jazz FM is the United Kingdom's national jazz station, blending jazz with soul and blues in a way designed to welcome newcomers without boring the faithful. Three decades after its founding it remains one of very few full-time commercial jazz broadcasters anywhere in the world.

## History

Jazz FM launched on 4 March 1990 on 102.2 FM in London — the first legal station in Britain dedicated to jazz. Its early years were turbulent, with owners repeatedly diluting the jazz content in search of ratings (a period that produced the memorable protest slogan "keep jazz on Jazz FM"). The station passed through several hands, spent years rebranded as Smooth, and was revived as a digital-first national service, finding a stable home when Bauer acquired it in 2018. Through every twist, its core identity — jazz presented with warmth rather than academic distance — has survived.

## Format and programming

Daytime programming leans on soulful, accessible jazz, vocalists and classic soul, while evenings and weekends dig deeper: bebop, contemporary UK jazz, Latin, funk and specialist blues shows. The station has been an important champion of Britain's young jazz renaissance, giving early airplay to artists like Ezra Collective and Nubya Garcia, and its annual Jazz FM Awards became a fixture of the UK scene.

## Reach

Jazz FM broadcasts nationally on DAB and streams through its app and website, with premium ad-free options for devotees.

## Significance

Jazz FM keeps an entire genre visible on British commercial radio. Its persistence through decades of ownership drama — and its role in amplifying the current golden generation of UK jazz — make it a quietly heroic institution in European specialist broadcasting.
