CJAD 800 is the news and talk voice of English-speaking Montreal. For Quebec's anglophone minority it is more than a radio station — it has served for decades as the community's town square, information lifeline and collective memory.

## History

CJAD was founded in 1945 by broadcaster J. Arthur Dupont, whose initials gave the station its call letters. It grew into Montreal's dominant English station through the second half of the twentieth century, covering the city's dramatic decades — Expo 67, the October Crisis, two sovereignty referendums — from the particular vantage point of its English-speaking audience. Long owned by Standard Broadcasting and then Astral, it joined Bell Media in 2013 and anchors the company's Montreal English cluster.

## Format and programming

The schedule is talk from morning to night: the Andrew Carter morning show, midday and afternoon phone-ins on city issues, consumer and legal advice hours, and long-serving hosts who become family to their audience. News coverage emphasizes Montreal city hall, Quebec politics and the questions of language and identity that shape anglophone life in the province.

## Reach

CJAD broadcasts on 800 AM across greater Montreal and streams via iHeartRadio Canada. Within its community its penetration is remarkable — among English-speaking Montrealers it has ranked first for generations.

## Significance

CJAD demonstrates radio's role as an institution for a linguistic minority: it binds together a community of under a million people inside a francophone metropolis, and its hosts and callers have conducted the anglophone side of Quebec's great debates for nearly eighty years.
