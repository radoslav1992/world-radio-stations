Skyrock is France's leading rap and R&B station and one of the most consequential urban-music broadcasters in the world. Its slogan — "Premier sur le Rap" ("first on rap") — is both marketing and plain fact: no broadcaster did more to make hip hop a mainstream French music.

## History

Pierre Bellanger launched Skyrock in 1986 on the free-radio wave that followed the end of the French state monopoly, evolving it from earlier pirate ventures. Initially a provocative pop station, Skyrock made its historic pivot in the mid-1990s, committing wholesale to French rap when the genre was still treated with suspicion — betting on artists like IAM, NTM and MC Solaar, and later launching the careers of a generation from Diam's to Booba. The nightly free-talk show "Radio Libre" became a rite of passage for young listeners, and in the 2000s the Skyblog platform made Skyrock briefly one of the largest social networks in Europe.

## Format and programming

Daytimes rotate French and international rap and R&B hits; nights belong to freestyle sessions, new-music premieres and the unfiltered talk that built the station's bond with its audience. Skyrock's playlist power remains decisive — French rap's dominance of the national charts owes much to its rotations.

## Reach

Skyrock broadcasts on FM across France, on DAB+ and online, consistently ranking among the country's top music stations, with an audience skewing younger than any competitor of its size.

## Significance

Skyrock did for French rap what no American station quite did for its own: it made a single broadcaster the genre's national institution. French hip hop's status as the world's second rap market is inseparable from this station.
