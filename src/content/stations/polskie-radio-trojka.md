Trójka — the Third Programme of Polish public radio — is one of the most culturally beloved stations in Central Europe. For decades it was where Poles heard rock music, literate satire and honest conversation, a station whose DJs were trusted guides through both music and difficult times.

## History

Trójka launched on 1 April 1962 and found its calling in the 1970s and 80s as the freest-sounding voice inside communist Poland's official media: Western rock, jazz and cabaret-style humour delivered with a wink the censors couldn't quite pin down. Its songs chart — the Lista Przebojów Programu Trzeciego, or LP3, launched in 1982 and long curated by the legendary Marek Niedźwiecki — became a national institution whose weekly countdown listeners planned evenings around. In 2020 political interference in a chart result triggered resignations of famous hosts and a listener exodus to newly founded independent stations — a crisis that itself testified to how much the station means to Poles. Trójka has since worked to rebuild its identity.

## Format and programming

The schedule mixes rock, alternative and singer-songwriter music with cultural magazines, radio drama, reportage — a proud Polish Radio art form — and the humour segments that produced some of the country's most quoted comedy. The LP3 chart still runs, counting among the longest-lived music charts in European radio.

## Reach

Trójka broadcasts nationwide on FM and DAB+ and streams online, its audience loyal if smaller than in its ratings heyday.

## Significance

Trójka shaped the musical and comedic sensibility of several Polish generations, and its history — from sly resistance under communism to the 2020 storm — is a running referendum on the independence of public media in Poland.
