NRK P3 is the youth channel of Norway's public broadcaster — the station charged with keeping NRK relevant to listeners under thirty, and unusually, one run not from Oslo but from Trondheim. It has launched much of modern Norwegian pop and most of the country's radio comedy.

## History

P3 went on air in 1993 when NRK split its radio output into three distinct channels, and the youth network was deliberately planted in Trondheim's Tyholt district, away from the capital's media gravity. The distance became identity: P3 developed a looser, funnier, riskier culture that produced beloved comedy shows and personalities who went on to dominate Norwegian television. Its unsigned-artist platform "Urørt" ("Untouched") became the standard route from bedroom demo to festival stage, and the annual P3 Gull awards celebrate the Norwegian music the channel spends all year championing.

## Format and programming

Daytimes carry current pop, hip hop and Norwegian releases with youth-focused news from NRK's newsroom; evenings dig into specialist scenes. Comedy and talk remain central — P3's presenter alumni read like a who's-who of Norwegian entertainment — and its documentary podcasts extend the brand to audiences who rarely touch live radio. Norway's pioneering 2017 switch-off of national FM made P3 an all-digital station earlier than almost any peer worldwide.

## Reach

P3 broadcasts nationwide on DAB+ and streams through NRK Radio, with sister streams P3X and mP3 serving narrower tastes.

## Significance

P3 is the institution that keeps Norwegian public broadcasting young: talent scout for the country's music, nursery for its comedians, and a case study in thriving after FM.
