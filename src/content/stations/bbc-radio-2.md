BBC Radio 2 is the BBC's national adult contemporary station, offering a broad mix of popular music, entertainment, documentaries and specialist programming aimed at listeners over 35. It regularly tops UK audience figures, making it the country's most popular radio station.

## History

Radio 2 launched on 30 September 1967 as the successor to the BBC Light Programme, part of the corporation's wholesale reorganisation of its radio networks that also created Radio 1, Radio 3 and Radio 4. In its early decades it was known for easy listening and light entertainment, gradually evolving from the 1990s onwards into a contemporary music station with high-profile presenters drawn from television and pop radio. Its breakfast show, hosted over the years by figures such as Terry Wogan, Chris Evans and Zoe Ball, has long been among the most listened-to programmes in British radio.

## Format and programming

The station's daytime output centres on melodic pop from the 1970s to the present, presented by well-known personalities. Beyond the mainstream playlist, Radio 2 carries an unusually wide range of specialist music programming, including shows dedicated to folk, country, jazz, blues, soul and musical theatre, along with documentaries, comedy and live sessions. Flagship events include coverage of major music festivals and its own live concerts.

## Reach

Radio 2 broadcasts across the United Kingdom on FM between 88 and 90.2 MHz and on DAB digital radio, and streams worldwide through the BBC Sounds platform. It routinely records the largest weekly audience of any UK radio station, reaching well over ten million listeners.

## Significance

As the BBC's biggest station by audience, Radio 2 occupies a central place in British broadcasting. It serves as a bridge between mainstream pop culture and specialist music traditions, and its presenters and programmes are fixtures of national life. The station is also an important platform for established and heritage artists, with live sessions and concerts forming a core part of its identity.
