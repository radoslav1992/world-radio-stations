ABC Radio National — universally "RN" — is Australia's network of ideas: a national public station devoted to documentaries, science, history, books, religion and unhurried conversation. It is the closest thing the southern hemisphere has to BBC Radio 4.

## History

RN descends from the Australian Broadcasting Commission's original national networks of the 1930s and 40s, which carried talks, drama and education alongside news across the continent. Reorganized and renamed over the decades — Radio 2, then Radio National — it became the ABC's dedicated home for depth once music and news moved to specialist networks. Programs of astonishing longevity anchor its identity: Robyn Williams has presented "The Science Show" since 1975, and Phillip Adams hosted "Late Night Live" for over three decades, interviewing seemingly everyone on earth at least twice.

## Format and programming

The schedule is wall-to-wall speech: flagship current-affairs breakfasts, "Big Ideas" lectures, the religion and ethics coverage that is an RN specialty, literary programs, radio documentaries and some of the world's most respected science broadcasting. Much of its output leads a double life as highly successful podcasts — "Conversations", shared with ABC local radio, is perennially Australia's most downloaded.

## Reach

RN broadcasts on AM and FM transmitters covering the entire country — from capital cities to remote outback communities — plus DAB+ and the ABC listen app, which carries its programs worldwide.

## Significance

RN is Australia's intellectual commons. For listeners scattered across a vast continent it has functioned for generations as a national university of the air, and its documentary and science traditions have influenced public radio well beyond Australia.
