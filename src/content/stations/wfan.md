WFAN, "The Fan", is New York City's all-sports radio station and the outlet that invented the format. Since flipping to sports talk in 1987 it has been the model for hundreds of sports stations worldwide and a fixture of New York's famously opinionated sports culture.

## History

WFAN launched on 1 July 1987 as the world's first 24-hour all-sports radio station, initially at 1050 AM before moving in 1988 to the historic 660 AM frequency previously occupied by WNBC. The gamble that a major market could sustain round-the-clock sports conversation paid off spectacularly: by the 1990s WFAN was among the highest-billing stations in America. The afternoon pairing of Mike Francesa and Chris "Mad Dog" Russo, which ran from 1989 to 2008, became the most famous sports talk show in the country. An FM simulcast on 101.9 was added in 2012, and the station is now owned by Audacy.

## Format and programming

The schedule is wall-to-wall sports conversation built on caller interaction — long-running morning and afternoon shows, overnight open lines and constant debate about the Yankees, Mets, Giants, Jets, Knicks and Rangers. WFAN has also served as the play-by-play flagship for several of New York's major teams, including long associations with the Mets and later the Yankees.

## Reach

WFAN broadcasts on 660 AM, whose clear-channel signal covers much of the northeastern United States at night, and on 101.9 FM, with streaming through Audacy's platforms extending its reach nationwide.

## Significance

WFAN proved that sports talk could carry a full-time station in the biggest market in America, spawning an entire industry. Its blend of expertise, argument and caller theatre established the grammar of sports radio used everywhere from Boston to Sydney.
