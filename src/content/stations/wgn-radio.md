WGN Radio, on 720 AM, is Chicago's great heritage station — a full-service blend of news, talk, sports and personality that has narrated the city's life for a century. Its call letters stand for "World's Greatest Newspaper", the slogan of its founding owner, the Chicago Tribune.

## History

WGN traces its history to 1924, when the Tribune took over an existing Chicago station and stamped its brand on it. Through the twentieth century WGN was the definition of full-service radio: farm reports for the Midwest, big personalities in morning drive, and decades as the beloved flagship of the Chicago Cubs, whose games it carried from 1925 until 2014. Hosts like Wally Phillips — for years the most-listened-to man in Chicago — and agriculture broadcaster Orion Samuelson became regional institutions. The station passed from Tribune ownership to Nexstar Media Group in 2019.

## Format and programming

Today WGN programs news and personality talk with a distinctly Chicago sensibility — conversational rather than combative — alongside sports coverage, business updates and its long-running overnight shows, which have a devoted audience across the Midwest thanks to the station's huge nighttime signal.

## Reach

WGN's 50,000-watt clear-channel signal on 720 AM covers Chicagoland by day and much of central North America by night, with streaming and podcasts extending the brand further.

## Significance

Few stations have been as tightly woven into a city's identity. WGN shaped Chicago mornings, soundtracked generations of Cubs summers, and remains a rare surviving example of the full-service AM format that once dominated American radio.
