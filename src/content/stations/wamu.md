WAMU 88.5 is the leading public radio station of the American capital. Licensed to American University and an NPR member since the network's founding era, it combines NPR's national programming with Washington journalism and nationally distributed talk shows produced in its own studios.

## History

WAMU began FM broadcasting in 1961 from American University's campus, growing from a student-run operation into one of the country's most important public radio stations. For decades it was defined by "The Diane Rehm Show", the daily interview and call-in program that from Washington reached millions of listeners nationally through NPR distribution. When Rehm retired from daily radio in 2016, WAMU launched its successor "1A" with Joshua Johnson, which quickly became one of public radio's most carried daily programs. The station also has a curious side-history as a longtime home of bluegrass music on Washington's airwaves.

## Format and programming

WAMU's schedule pairs NPR staples — Morning Edition, All Things Considered — with its own productions, led by 1A's daily national conversation. Local reporting covers the District, Maryland and Virginia, with particular strength in transportation, housing and regional politics.

## Reach

Broadcasting on 88.5 FM across the Washington metropolitan area and streaming worldwide, WAMU consistently ranks among the region's most-listened stations — a public broadcaster competing at the top of a major commercial market.

## Significance

WAMU shows what a university-licensed station can become: a newsroom of record for a major region and a producer of national programming. Its interview franchises have set the tone for civil, substantive talk radio in America for over forty years.
