Radiožurnál is the news flagship of Český rozhlas, the Czech public broadcaster — the station Czechs turn to for breaking news, elections and the national conversation. It carries one of the proudest lineages in European radio.

## History

Czechoslovak Radio began regular broadcasting on 18 May 1923 from a tent in Prague's Kbely district — among the very first regular radio services in continental Europe. Its Vinohradská street headquarters became a literal battleground of Czech history: citizens died defending the building during the 1945 Prague Uprising and again when Warsaw Pact tanks arrived in August 1968, as announcers famously stayed on air describing the invasion until the last moment. Radiožurnál emerged as the modern news channel of the democratized broadcaster after 1989, and today anchors a public radio family spanning culture, music and regional networks.

## Format and programming

The station runs a disciplined news wheel through the day — bulletins, correspondent reports, interviews — with its lunchtime political interview a fixture that ministers cannot easily refuse. Sport matters too: Radiožurnál's live coverage of Czech ice hockey and football is appointment listening. A sister station, ČRo Plus, handles longer-form analysis, leaving Radiožurnál fast and accessible.

## Reach

Radiožurnál broadcasts nationwide on FM and DAB+ and streams via the mujRozhlas platform, consistently ranking among the country's most-listened stations — remarkable for a pure news format in a market of strong commercial music players.

## Significance

Radiožurnál embodies a century of Czech broadcasting — a service whose journalists have twice defended their microphones at gunpoint, now operating as one of Central Europe's most trusted news sources.
