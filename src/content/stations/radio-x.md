Radio X is the United Kingdom's biggest commercial rock and indie station, playing guitar music from Britpop's back catalogue to new alternative releases. It carries the DNA of its predecessor XFM — the London station that soundtracked the 1990s indie explosion — inside a national brand operated by Global.

## History

XFM launched full-time on London's 104.9 FM in 1997 after years of campaigning, dedicated to alternative music the mainstream dial neglected. It became legendary both for its playlist — championing Oasis, Blur, Radiohead and later The Strokes and The Libertines — and for its alumni: Ricky Gervais and Stephen Merchant's XFM shows with Karl Pilkington later became the world-conquering Ricky Gervais Show podcast. Global rebranded XFM as Radio X on 21 September 2015, hiring Chris Moyles, the former BBC Radio 1 breakfast titan, to front the relaunch. His show has since grown into one of UK commercial radio's biggest.

## Format and programming

Daytimes blend indie anthems — Oasis remain practically the house band — with current rock and alternative singles. Evening and specialist shows push newer guitar music, and the station's live sessions and gig presentations keep it close to the touring circuit.

## Reach

Radio X holds FM signals in London (104.9) and Manchester (97.7) and broadcasts nationally on DAB and Global Player, reaching an audience XFM could only have dreamed of.

## Significance

Radio X keeps guitar music commercially viable on national UK radio, and its XFM inheritance gives it real cultural credentials. The Moyles signing also proved that star presenters could move audiences from the BBC to commercial radio at scale.
