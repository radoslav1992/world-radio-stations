Radio Italia is the national station that plays Italian music and nothing else. Its promise — "solo musica italiana" — has held since 1982, making it the broadcast home of the country's singer-songwriters and pop stars and one of the most distinctive formats in European radio.

## History

Broadcaster Mario Volanti founded Radio Italia in Milan in 1982, gambling that in a market flooded with Anglo-American hits, an all-Italian playlist would stand out. The station grew from Lombard roots into a national network through the private-radio consolidation of the following decades, building unmatched relationships with Italian artists along the way. Its free open-air concerts, "Radio Italia Live", regularly fill Milan's Piazza del Duomo with crowds in the hundreds of thousands — among the largest recurring music events in Europe.

## Format and programming

The playlist spans the whole Italian songbook: the great cantautori — De Gregori, Venditti, Pausini's generation and beyond — alongside current pop, Sanremo Festival hits and emerging artists. Interviews and acoustic performances in the station's studios feed both radio and its television channel, Radio Italia TV, which simulcasts and extends the brand.

## Reach

Radio Italia broadcasts on FM across Italy, on DAB+ and via streaming, with its television and social channels making it a full Italian-music media house followed by the diaspora worldwide.

## Significance

Radio Italia is a cultural policy written as a business plan: by refusing foreign repertoire it became the reference platform for Italian song, a kingmaker for domestic artists and a genuine national institution — the sound of Italy, curated by Italians, for forty years.
