KQED is the principal public media organization of the San Francisco Bay Area, operating one of the most listened-to public radio stations in the United States. Its radio service on 88.5 FM combines national NPR programming with locally produced news, talk and cultural coverage.

## History

KQED Inc. was founded in 1954 as one of the earliest public broadcasters in the country, beginning with television before adding radio. KQED-FM went on the air in 1969 and grew alongside the organization's television and digital services into a comprehensive regional public media outlet. Over the decades the station built a large local newsroom and became a charter presence in Bay Area public life, supported primarily by member contributions.

## Format and programming

The station follows a news and talk format anchored by NPR staples such as Morning Edition and All Things Considered, complemented by KQED's own productions. Locally produced programs include Forum, a long-running daily call-in and interview show covering politics, culture and Bay Area issues. KQED's journalism spans state government, science, education and the arts, and much of it is distributed as podcasts and digital reporting in addition to broadcast.

## Reach

KQED broadcasts to the San Francisco Bay Area on 88.5 FM and reaches a wider audience through online streaming, podcasts and its website. It consistently ranks among the largest public radio audiences in the United States, reflecting both the size of its market and its strong local following.

## Significance

As the dominant public broadcaster in Northern California, KQED plays a central role in regional journalism and civic discourse. It is frequently cited as a model member-supported station, combining national public radio programming with an ambitious local news operation that has expanded as commercial local media in the region has contracted.
