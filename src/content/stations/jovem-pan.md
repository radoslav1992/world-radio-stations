Jovem Pan is one of the most famous names in Brazilian radio — a São Paulo institution that grew from a single AM station into a national media group spanning rolling news, opinion, sport and one of Latin America's biggest hit-music networks.

## History

Rádio Panamericana went on air in São Paulo in 1944, earning the nickname "Jovem Pan" ("Young Pan") in the 1960s as it chased Brazil's youth audience. Its sports department became legendary — for decades Jovem Pan's football coverage, with its operatic goal calls, defined São Paulo match days. Jovem Pan FM launched in the 1970s as the music arm and franchised its top-40 format to affiliates across Brazil, while the original news operation evolved into Jovem Pan News. In recent years the group has become one of Brazil's largest digital news broadcasters, its opinionated programs drawing enormous — and often controversial — audiences on YouTube.

## Format and programming

Jovem Pan FM plays Brazilian and international hits with irreverent personality shows and the humour franchises (most famously "Pânico") that made it a pop-culture factory. The news side runs morning agenda-setters, hard-edged opinion panels and sports debate, feeding broadcast, streaming and social platforms simultaneously.

## Reach

From flagship stations in São Paulo, the Jovem Pan brands reach most of Brazil through affiliate networks and one of the largest digital audiences of any radio-born media company in the Americas.

## Significance

Jovem Pan tracks the whole arc of Brazilian broadcasting: golden-age AM showmanship, FM pop dominance, and the leap of talk radio into digital video. Love it or argue with it — much of Brazil does both daily — it is impossible to ignore.
