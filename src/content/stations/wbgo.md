WBGO, "Jazz 88.3", is America's premier jazz radio station. Broadcasting from Newark, New Jersey, across the New York metropolitan area, it is a nonprofit, member-supported outlet devoted almost entirely to jazz — a rarity anywhere in the world, and an institution in the music's historic capital region.

## History

WBGO began broadcasting as a jazz station in 1979, when Newark Public Radio acquired the frequency from the city's board of education. From the start it positioned itself as a full-time home for jazz at a moment when commercial stations were abandoning the format. Over four decades it has built deep relationships with the New York jazz scene — musicians, clubs and labels — and become one of the most listened-to jazz stations on earth, with a membership base that sustains it without commercial advertising.

## Format and programming

The station plays jazz around the clock: hard bop, swing, vocal jazz, Latin jazz and blues, presented by hosts who are themselves respected figures in the community. WBGO has co-produced nationally distributed programming, including NPR's "Jazz Night in America" with Christian McBride, and its news team covers Newark and New Jersey public affairs, grounding the station in its home city.

## Reach

WBGO's 88.3 FM signal covers Newark, Manhattan and much of the New York metro area, while its stream and apps serve a worldwide audience of jazz listeners — for many of whom it functions as the reference jazz station regardless of where they live.

## Significance

As commercial jazz radio has all but vanished, WBGO's role has only grown: it is a primary broadcast platform for the world's most important jazz scene and a model of how member-supported radio can sustain a specialist art form. For jazz musicians, airplay and a session on WBGO remain a rite of passage.
