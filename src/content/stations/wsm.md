WSM is the most famous station in country music. Broadcasting from Nashville on 650 AM since 1925, it is the home of the Grand Ole Opry — the longest-running radio program in American history — and the station around which Nashville's entire music industry grew.

## History

WSM signed on 5 October 1925, owned by the National Life and Accident Insurance Company, whose slogan "We Shield Millions" gave the station its call letters. Weeks later, an old-time music program called the WSM Barn Dance took to the air; renamed the Grand Ole Opry in 1927, it has been broadcast every week since. WSM's powerful clear-channel signal carried the Opry across most of North America at night, drawing musicians to Nashville and effectively founding "Music City". The station's 878-foot diamond-shaped Blaw-Knox transmitter tower, erected in 1932, still stands south of Nashville and is a landmark of broadcast engineering.

## Format and programming

WSM programs classic and contemporary country with a depth no other station can match, alongside live sessions, bluegrass and Americana shows, and of course the Opry broadcasts every week. Its announcers carry on a continuous tradition of live country radio presentation stretching back a century.

## Reach

On 650 AM, WSM's 50,000-watt clear-channel signal reaches much of the eastern half of North America after dark, and its stream brings the Opry and Nashville's sound to a global audience.

## Significance

No radio station is more entwined with a musical genre. WSM built the institution — the Opry — that made Nashville the capital of country music, launched careers from Hank Williams to Garth Brooks, and remains the format's spiritual home. In 2025 the station marked a full century on the air.
